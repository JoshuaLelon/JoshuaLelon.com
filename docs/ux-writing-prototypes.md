# finance examples for the writing and exploration prototypes

research and proposal, november 16, 2026. prototype 1 is implemented as a staged interaction with authored text; the remaining entries are proposed experiments. none establishes live model quality.

renting versus buying is the default example. use broader everyday finance when it makes the interaction easier to understand. consistency comes from the interaction vocabulary, reused facts, and common description fields; it does not require putting a house into every demonstration.

all 14 descriptions use the same fields: title, example, question, starting state, interaction, visible result, comparison, success criterion, and connections. the example field makes departures from the default explicit. connections identify useful combinations, not mandatory implementation dependencies.

**shared example: sam is considering renting or buying**

central writing prompt: **the rent and mortgage payments match. what else matters?** the essay examines monthly cash requirements, upfront money, ownership and equity, and the possibility of moving. its conclusion is conditional; the initial figures alone do not determine which choice is better.

| input | fictional value used across the demos |
| --- | --- |
| rent | $2,000 per month |
| purchase price | $400,000 |
| down payment | $80,000 |
| purchase closing costs | $10,000, separate from the down payment |
| mortgage payment | $2,000 per month for principal and interest; stipulated for the example, with no rate or term inferred |
| property tax | $400 per month |
| homeowners insurance | $100 per month |
| maintenance provision | $300 set aside per month; a budget allowance, not a known repair bill |
| monthly ownership budget | $2,800 including the maintenance provision; $800 above the stated rent |
| available savings | $110,000 before purchase; $20,000 after the down payment and purchase closing costs |
| expected stay | three years initially; a ten-year alternative is used when exploring assumptions |
| unresolved inputs | sale proceeds and selling costs, rent changes, actual repairs, loan balance over time, renter-specific costs, and the return on money retained while renting |

these are authored demonstration inputs, not current market estimates. distinguish cash requirements from economic cost: a down payment is not all an expense, principal repayment is not all an expense, and an unspent maintenance reserve remains savings. the $800 gap compares the stated monthly budgets; it does not establish an overall cost difference, a break-even year, or a recommended choice.

**other examples, used only where useful**

| example | shared material | why use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| compound interest | an illustrative $1,000 balance earning a fixed 10% annually becomes $1,100 after one year and $1,210 after two; no contributions, withdrawals, fees, or taxes in this simplified example | a compact explanation with natural levels of detail, multiple ways to explain it, and a chronological sequence |
| spreading investment risk | compare money concentrated in one company with money spread across several companies; include a hypothetical event affecting all of them | supplies definitions, examples, counterexamples, and relationship diagrams without needing mortgage mechanics |
| explaining a budget | a $600 insurance bill covers a year, equivalent to a $50 monthly provision | lets the writer correct an assumption during generation and see precisely what must change |

**behavior shared across the prototypes**

| distinction | behavior |
| --- | --- |
| scope versus detail | selecting a paragraph chooses an editing target; showing its summary changes how much is visible |
| preview versus apply | browsing an alternative leaves the committed draft intact until it is applied |
| alternative versus history | candidates are possible choices; saved versions record actual states |
| medium versus structure | essay/thread/dialogue and comparison/problem–solution remain separate choices |
| grouping versus identity | comparison groups and labels can be recomputed; current selections and saved passages remain addressable |
| reading, writing, exploring | reading opens explanations; writing proposes edits; exploring records a navigable path through possibilities |
| facts versus assumptions | the scenario's fixed inputs, proposed assumptions, and inferred conclusions remain distinguishable |

**fit with this project**

- implement each later as an independent native astro/mdx prototype, using the existing `/prototypes` collection and component layout.
- show only title, clickable tags, and the demo/walkthrough component in the gallery. on the dedicated page, include a breadcrumb, title, short description, tags, component, then problem solved, interface inspirations, interaction patterns, and design decisions. keep this research document outside the public prototype body.
- retain the centered previous/next walkthrough below the component. show one step at a time and require the step's actual action before next becomes available. previous must restore a coherent earlier state.
- use only controls necessary to the particular interaction. general timing sliders, animation inspectors, and a global variant picker are not part of the gallery shell.
- share scenario data within each example and declare any alternate assumptions explicitly. use authored candidate sets in the first pass. the prototype must expose genuine interaction states, but cannot establish live model quality or latency until those are tested separately.
- preserve selection and focus, support keyboard and touch, avoid gesture capture outside an explicitly active surface, and provide a reduced-motion path. walkthrough navigation can animate when the motion is the subject of the demonstration; ordinary keyboard navigation should remain immediate.
- test long prose, a missing candidate, conflicting constraints, repeated input, interrupted movement, and returning to earlier state. compare design alternatives on the same material.
- the eventual shared model needs stable identities for content and selections, disposable group labels, separate saved versions and view state, and explicit proposed versus applied edits. building that entire model is not a prerequisite for the first isolated experiment.
