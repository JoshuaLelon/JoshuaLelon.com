## problem solved

sometimes i can recognize what i want long before i can write a prompt for it. here, choosing *story* gives me a premise to react to. i can keep answering the suggested question, interrupt to work on a character, or select the exact passage i want to change. the walkthrough follows one writer making a mystery about the last letter in a village postbox. previous and next replay the writer’s decisions. scroll anywhere over the question panel to browse its answers. you can also swipe, drag, or use the left and right arrow keys while the answers have focus. open a question mark to see a definition, an example, and where the idea comes from. each caption explains the action shown on the following step.

## interface inspirations

- a reader can open one part of a sentence and keep reading around it in [joe davis’s telescopic text](https://interfacelineage.com/telescopic-text). here, opening one beat lets me try its prose while its neighbors still fit on the page.
- choosing a folder reveals its children in the [finder’s column view](https://interfacelineage.com/miller-columns). the selection browser borrows that drill-down gesture. choosing “one passage” reveals each eligible passage by name.

## interaction patterns

- **disclosure.** the draft has room for a premise, an outline, and individual passages. choosing “rise, fall, rise” drafts three acts beneath the premise. the chevron folds or reopens that outline. a sparkle beside each act drafts its scene. [disclosure pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/)
- **hierarchical selection.** at the farthest level you choose a group. one level down you can inspect its members. the selected passages stay highlighted in the document while you decide. [miller columns](https://interfacelineage.com/miller-columns)

## design decisions

- **take the question and its answers from the same framework.** genre establishes that nell is in a ghost story. the next question asks about emotional arc. a fall then rise could take nell from the pain of recognizing her mother’s handwriting to the relief of getting an answer. when the writer asks about nell instead, the editor draws its choices from a character framework. <a href={storyFrameworks.emotionalArc.url}>the six emotional arcs</a> · <a href={storyFrameworks.characterArchetype.url}>dramatica’s eight character archetypes</a>
- **show where the draft is changing.** a cursor traces each choice. three short lines of ink mark an empty passage while it is being drafted. when a passage already has words, they dim until the revision is ready. the old wording lifts away as the new words settle into the same lines. questions change in the same spot, with the microphone and surrounding controls staying in view. the prose fades in first, followed by its bracket and label, so you can read the sentence before classifying it. the demo simulates the wait for generation.
- **keep facts visible.** the genre bracket spans the premise and every open act. each act has its own bracket and a dropdown for its emotional direction. choosing mystery in the genre dropdown rewrites the ghost story’s premise. nell’s archetype dropdown sits above her name and follows it as the premise changes. you can see what the writer specified before reading the resulting prose.
- **keep two questions within reach.** selecting a passage gives you a local decision. the suggested next decision can move to another passage or back to the whole document.
- **give browsing and expansion separate controls.** close and far change which selections or questions you can browse. the chevron changes how much of the saved draft you can read. “draft another outline” is a separate choice for making an alternate.
- **put alternatives back in context.** choose a rhetorical form and read its wording in the same passage. choose the earlier form to bring its version back.
