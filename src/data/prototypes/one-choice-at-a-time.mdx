## problem solved

sometimes i can recognize what i want long before i can write a prompt for it. here, choosing *story* gives me a premise to react to. i can keep answering the suggested question, interrupt to work on a character, or select the exact passage i want to change. the walkthrough follows one writer making a mystery about the last letter in a village postbox. previous and next replay the writer’s decisions. a cursor shows where the writer scrolls. over the answer row you can also browse the choices yourself. open a question mark to see a definition, an example, and where the idea comes from. each caption explains the action shown on the following step.

## interface inspirations

- a reader can open one part of a sentence and keep reading around it in [joe davis’s telescopic text](https://interfacelineage.com/telescopic-text). here, opening one beat lets me try its prose while its neighbors still fit on the page.
- choosing a folder reveals its children in the [finder’s column view](https://interfacelineage.com/miller-columns). the question panel borrows that drill-down gesture. “point of view” opens the choice of narrator. the active question and its answers sit in the center. the questions on either side are its peers. above is the broader question, and below is the question opened by the current branch choice.

## interaction patterns

- **disclosure.** the draft has room for a premise, an outline, and individual passages. choosing “rise, fall, rise” replaces the premise with three act summaries one level deeper. each expansion indents the children one level from the summary they replace. the chevron brings the premise back. scrolling down over act i replaces its summary with prose. another downward scroll replaces nell’s opening sentence with the entry in her collection book. the sparkle beside act iii opens a new scene there. [disclosure pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/)
- **question hierarchy.** scrolling up from a question reveals its siblings as choices. the question you came from is highlighted. scroll across to another choice, then down to open it. [miller columns](https://interfacelineage.com/miller-columns)

## design decisions

- **take the question and its answers from the same framework.** genre establishes that nell is in a ghost story. the next question asks about emotional arc. a fall then rise could take nell from the pain of recognizing her mother’s handwriting to the relief of getting an answer. when the writer asks about nell instead, the editor draws its choices from a character framework. <a href={storyFrameworks.emotionalArc.url}>the six emotional arcs</a> · <a href={storyFrameworks.characterArchetype.url}>dramatica’s eight character archetypes</a>
- **show where the draft is changing.** a cursor traces each choice. three short lines of ink mark an empty passage while it is being drafted. when a passage already has words, they dim until the revision is ready. the old wording lifts away as the new words settle into the same lines. questions change in the same spot, with the microphone and surrounding controls staying in view. the prose fades in first, followed by its bracket and label, so you can read the sentence before classifying it. the demo simulates the wait for generation.
- **keep facts visible.** the genre bracket spans the visible story, from a single premise to all three acts. each act has its own bracket and a dropdown for its emotional direction. choosing mystery in the genre dropdown rewrites the ghost story’s premise. nell’s archetype dropdown sits above her name and follows it as the premise changes. you can see what the writer specified before reading the resulting prose.
- **keep two questions within reach.** selecting a passage gives you a local decision. the suggested next decision can move to another passage or back to the whole document.
- **select by description.** in the walkthrough, the writer presses the microphone beneath the draft and says “the part where the baker moves the tray.” the sentence where he slides the tray aside lights up.
- **let the pointer set the scope.** across the answer row, the writer browses answers. across the heading, they browse questions. in the margin, a sideways scroll adds a related sentence and a vertical scroll jumps straight to a different selection. over the prose, scrolling up folds the scene containing the first selected sentence. scrolling down reopens it with the same words highlighted. when the selected leaf has no saved children, the same scroll generates the next level.
- **put alternatives back in context.** choose a rhetorical form and read its wording in the same passage. choose the earlier form to bring its version back.
